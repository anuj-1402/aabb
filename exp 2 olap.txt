# PART A — THEORY

## 1. What is OLAP?

**OLAP** stands for **Online Analytical Processing**.

OLAP is a technology used to analyze large amounts of data from different perspectives or dimensions.

It is mainly used in:

- Business Intelligence
- Data Warehousing
- Reporting
- Decision making
- Sales analysis
- Financial analysis
- Customer analysis
- Trend analysis

The main purpose of OLAP is not to record transactions, but to **analyze existing data**.

### Simple example

Suppose a company has millions of sales transactions.

Management may want to ask:

> "How much did we sell in Mumbai?"

Then:

> "How much did we sell in Mumbai for Electronics?"

Then:

> "How much did we sell in Mumbai for Electronics during Q1?"

OLAP operations allow us to look at the same data from these different perspectives.

---

# 2. OLTP vs OLAP

Before understanding OLAP, it is important to understand the difference between **OLTP** and **OLAP**.

### OLTP — Online Transaction Processing

OLTP systems are used for **day-to-day transactions**.

Examples:

- Placing an order
- Making a payment
- Booking a ticket
- Updating customer information
- Recording a sale

Example:

```text
Customer buys Laptop
        ↓
Transaction recorded
        ↓
Payment processed
        ↓
Inventory updated
```

OLTP focuses on **individual transactions**.

---

### OLAP — Online Analytical Processing

OLAP takes historical data and analyzes it.

Example:

```text
Millions of Sales Transactions
              ↓
          Data Warehouse
              ↓
             OLAP
              ↓
       Business Analysis
              ↓
 "Which city has the highest sales?"
```

### Difference

| OLTP | OLAP |
|---|---|
| Transaction processing | Analytical processing |
| Current data | Historical data |
| Many small transactions | Large analytical queries |
| Insert/Update/Delete | Mostly Read |
| Highly normalized | Usually dimensional |
| Used by operational users | Used by analysts/managers |
| Example: Banking transaction | Example: Yearly sales analysis |

---

# 3. OLAP Cube

The central concept behind OLAP is the **multidimensional data cube**.

A data cube represents data using multiple **dimensions** and **measures**.

For example, consider sales.

### Dimensions

We could analyze sales using:

```text
Time
Product
Location
```

### Measure

The measurable value is:

```text
Sales Amount
```

So conceptually:

```text
                 TIME
                  ↑
                  |
                  |
                  |
                  +----------------→ PRODUCT
                 /
                /
               /
          LOCATION
```

Each combination of dimensions contains a measure.

For example:

```text
Mumbai + Electronics + 2025
              ↓
          ₹75,000
```

Another:

```text
Pune + Furniture + 2025
              ↓
          ₹45,000
```

The cube allows us to move through different levels of detail.

---

# 4. Dimensions

A **dimension** is a perspective or category through which data can be analyzed.

Common dimensions include:

### Time Dimension

```text
Year
 ↓
Quarter
 ↓
Month
 ↓
Day
```

### Location Dimension

```text
Country
 ↓
State
 ↓
City
 ↓
Store
```

### Product Dimension

```text
Category
 ↓
Subcategory
 ↓
Product
```

For example:

```text
Electronics
    ↓
Computers
    ↓
Laptop
```

Dimensions provide the **context** for measures.

---

# 5. Measures

A **measure** is a numerical value that can be analyzed or aggregated.

Examples:

- SalesAmount
- Quantity
- Profit
- Revenue
- Cost
- Discount

For example:

```text
Product       Quantity       Sales
Laptop           5          ₹2,75,000
Mouse           20          ₹16,000
Keyboard        10          ₹15,000
```

Here:

```text
Quantity → Measure
Sales    → Measure
```

# 6. Dimension Hierarchy

A dimension hierarchy represents different levels of detail.

For example, Time:

```text
Year
 │
 ├── Quarter
 │
 ├── Month
 │
 └── Day
```

More accurately:

```text
2025
 │
 ├── Q1
 │    ├── January
 │    ├── February
 │    └── March
 │
 └── Q2
      ├── April
      ├── May
      └── June
```

This hierarchy is extremely important for **Drill-down and Roll-up**.

### Drill-down

Move:

```text
Year → Quarter → Month → Day
```

More detail.

### Roll-up

Move:

```text
Day → Month → Quarter → Year
```

More summarized.

# 7. OLAP Operations

The five important OLAP operations in this experiment are:

1. **Slice**
2. **Dice**
3. **Drill-down**
4. **Roll-up**
5. **Pivot**

They all analyze the same underlying data but from different perspectives.

---

# 8. Slice Operation

## Definition

**Slice** is an OLAP operation that selects a **single value from one dimension**, producing a smaller view of the data.

In simple words:

> Fix one dimension to one particular value and examine the remaining data.

---

### Example

Suppose we have:

```text
Year
City
Product
Sales
```

If we select:

```text
Year = 2025
```

we have sliced the cube at 2025.

Conceptually:

```text
3D Cube

       Year
        |
        | 2024
        | 2025  ← SELECT
        | 2026
        |
        +----------------
```

The result is a smaller dataset containing only 2025.

### Important point

**Slice generally selects one fixed value from one dimension.**

Example:

```text
Year = 2025
```

not:

```text
Year = 2025 AND City = Mumbai
```

That starts becoming a **Dice** operation because multiple dimensions are being restricted.

---

# 9. Dice Operation

## Definition

**Dice** selects a **sub-cube** by applying conditions to multiple dimensions.

In simple words:

> Dice selects specific values or ranges from two or more dimensions.

---

### Example

Suppose we select:

```text
Year = 2025
City = Mumbai OR Pune
Product Category = Electronics
```

We have restricted multiple dimensions.

Therefore:

```text
Year       → 2025
City       → Mumbai, Pune
Category   → Electronics
```

This produces a smaller sub-cube.

### Slice vs Dice

| Slice | Dice |
|---|---|
| Usually one dimension is fixed | Multiple dimensions are restricted |
| Produces a slice of cube | Produces a sub-cube |
| Example: Year = 2025 | Example: Year = 2025, City = Mumbai/Pune, Category = Electronics |

Easy way to remember:

> **Slice = one cut**  
> **Dice = multiple cuts**

---

# 10. Drill-down Operation

## Definition

**Drill-down** moves from higher-level summarized data to lower-level detailed data.

It means:

> Going from general information to more detailed information.

---

### Time example

```text
Year
 ↓
Quarter
 ↓
Month
 ↓
Day
```

Suppose initially we have:

```text
2025 → ₹10,00,000
```

Drill down:

```text
2025
  ↓
Q1 → ₹2,50,000
Q2 → ₹3,00,000
Q3 → ₹2,00,000
Q4 → ₹2,50,000
```

Drill down again:

```text
Q1
 ↓
January  → ₹80,000
February → ₹90,000
March    → ₹80,000
```

So:

**Drill-down = more detail.**

---

# 11. Roll-up Operation

## Definition

**Roll-up** is the opposite of drill-down.

It moves from detailed data to summarized data.

Example:

```text
Day
 ↓
Month
 ↓
Quarter
 ↓
Year
```

Suppose we have:

```text
January  → ₹80,000
February → ₹90,000
March    → ₹80,000
```

Roll-up:

```text
Q1 → ₹2,50,000
```

Roll-up can also be performed across geographical hierarchies.

For example:

```text
Store
 ↓
City
 ↓
State
 ↓
Country
```

So:

```text
Store-level sales
       ↓
City-level sales
       ↓
State-level sales
       ↓
Country-level sales
```

### Important difference

```text
DRILL-DOWN
Less detail → More detail

ROLL-UP
More detail → Less detail
```

---

# 12. Pivot Operation

## Definition

**Pivot** changes the orientation or arrangement of dimensions in an OLAP report.

It does **not necessarily change the underlying data**.

It simply changes how the information is displayed.

For example:

### Before Pivot

```text
             Product
City          Laptop    Mouse
--------------------------------
Mumbai        55000     1600
Pune          70000     2400
Nashik        45000     800
```

After pivot:

```text
             City
Product       Mumbai    Pune    Nashik
---------------------------------------
Laptop        55000    70000    45000
Mouse          1600     2400      800
```

The information is the same.

The rows and columns have been exchanged.

### Simple definition

> **Pivot = rotate/rearrange the dimensions of the report.**

---

# 13. Summary of OLAP Operations

| Operation | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Slice** | Selects one value of a dimension | Year = 2025 |
| **Dice** | Selects values from multiple dimensions | 2025 + Mumbai/Pune + Electronics |
| **Drill-down** | Moves to more detail | Year → Quarter → Month |
| **Roll-up** | Moves to higher summary | Day → Month → Quarter → Year |
| **Pivot** | Changes report orientation | Rows ↔ Columns |

# 16. Drill-down Operation

Now let's say management initially wants **sales by year**.

We aggregate our data.

### Level 1 — Year

For 2025:

```text
55000 + 60000 + 1600 + 2400 + 4500 + 5000
+ 55000 + 60000 + 1600 + 2400 + 4500 + 5000
```

= **₹260,000**

For 2026:

```text
65000 + 70000 + 1800 + 2500
```

= **₹139,300**

So the summarized table is:

| Year | Total Sales |
|---:|---:|
| 2025 | ₹260,000 |
| 2026 | ₹139,300 |

This is our high-level view.

---

## Drill down: Year → Quarter

Now we want more detail.

```text
Year
 ↓
Quarter
```

### Result

| Year | Quarter | Total Sales |
|---:|---|---:|
| 2025 | Q1 | ₹128,500 |
| 2025 | Q2 | ₹131,500 |
| 2026 | Q1 | ₹139,300 |

Now we can see which quarter generated the sales.

---

## Drill down again: Quarter → Month

For 2025 Q1:

| Year | Quarter | Month | Sales |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 2025 | Q1 | Jan | ₹115,000 |
| 2025 | Q1 | Feb | ₹4,000 |
| 2025 | Q1 | Mar | ₹9,500 |

So our hierarchy is:

```text
Year
  ↓
Quarter
  ↓
Month
  ↓
Day
```

Every downward movement gives us **more detailed information**.

### Therefore:

> **Drill-down = moving from summarized data toward detailed data.**

---

# 17. Roll-up Operation

Now do exactly the opposite.

Suppose we start with monthly data:

| Year | Quarter | Month | Sales |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 2025 | Q1 | Jan | ₹115,000 |
| 2025 | Q1 | Feb | ₹4,000 |
| 2025 | Q1 | Mar | ₹9,500 |
| 2025 | Q2 | Apr | ₹115,000 |
| 2025 | Q2 | May | ₹4,000 |
| 2025 | Q2 | Jun | ₹9,500 |

We roll up:

```text
Month
 ↓
Quarter
```

### Result

| Year | Quarter | Sales |
|---:|---|---:|
| 2025 | Q1 | ₹128,500 |
| 2025 | Q2 | ₹131,500 |

Then roll up again:

```text
Quarter
 ↓
Year
```

### Result

| Year | Total Sales |
|---:|---:|
| 2025 | ₹260,000 |

So:

```text
DAY
 ↓
MONTH
 ↓
QUARTER
 ↓
YEAR
```

is a **roll-up hierarchy**.

### Therefore:

> **Roll-up = moving from detailed data toward summarized data.**

# 18. Pivot Operation

Now let's analyze sales by city and product.

Initially we can represent it as:

| City | Product | Sales |
|---|---|---:|
| Mumbai | Laptop | 55000 |
| Pune | Laptop | 60000 |
| Mumbai | Mouse | 1600 |
| Pune | Mouse | 2400 |
| Mumbai | Chair | 4500 |
| Pune | Chair | 5000 |

Now we **pivot** the data.

### Before Pivot

```text
City → Product → Sales
```

### After Pivot

We make:

```text
             Laptop    Mouse    Chair
Mumbai        55000     1600     4500
Pune          60000     2400     5000
```

So the resulting report becomes:

| City | Laptop | Mouse | Chair |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mumbai | ₹55,000 | ₹1,600 | ₹4,500 |
| Pune | ₹60,000 | ₹2,400 | ₹5,000 |

Now let's reverse the orientation.

### Pivot again

Products become rows:

| Product | Mumbai | Pune |
|---|---:|---:|
| Laptop | ₹55,000 | ₹60,000 |
| Mouse | ₹1,600 | ₹2,400 |
| Chair | ₹4,500 | ₹5,000 |

The data itself hasn't changed.

Only its **orientation/presentation** changed.
